# DM42 Applied Problem Solving

An example-driven handbook for the **SwissMicros DM42**, focused on practical scientific, mathematical, engineering, and programmer-oriented problem solving.

## Download

[Download the complete PDF](./DM42_Applied_Problem_Solving.pdf)

The handbook is formatted for A4 printing and contains 39 pages.

## About the book

This is not a basic calculator manual or an introduction to RPN. It assumes ordinary RPN fluency and concentrates on structured problems: choosing a model, entering the calculation correctly, checking the result, and recognising numerical or physical mistakes.

Each worked example combines:

- a clearly defined practical problem;
- the relevant mathematical or physical model;
- a reproducible DM42 procedure;
- numerical and dimensional checks; and
- cautions about assumptions, precision, units, and calculator modes.

## Topics covered

- Memory, scaling, and scientific notation
- Geometry, trajectories, motion, rotation, and orbital calculations
- Oscillations, gases, optics, and photon energy
- DC electronics and transient circuits
- Complex numbers, phasors, and AC circuits
- Matrices and simultaneous equations
- Numerical root finding and integration
- Experimental data, regression, statistics, and uncertainty
- Probability, finance, and workshop mathematics
- Numerical differentiation and general numerical judgement

## DM42 for programmers

The expanded programmer addendum covers:

- hexadecimal, binary, octal, and fixed-width integer representations;
- signed, unsigned, wrapped, and non-wrapped arithmetic;
- masks, bit fields, shifts, rotations, carry, and overflow;
- packing, unpacking, byte order, and sign extension;
- parity, XOR checksums, and a worked CRC-8 example;
- IEEE 754 binary32 and binary64 field layouts; and
- character codes and compact programming challenges.

## Intended audience

The book is for DM42 owners who already understand the calculator's basic operation and want realistic worked examples for science, electronics, engineering, workshop calculations, or low-level programming analysis.
